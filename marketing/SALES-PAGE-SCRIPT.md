## 3. What it actually does

Four blocks. Each is a real panel in the plugin — pair each with a
screenshot.

**Read the beat**
> Drop a beat in, or just let the session play. KeyGlo returns the key,
> the scale, the tempo and how far off concert pitch the whole track sits.
> If the material is pure drums, it says **No Reliable Key** instead of
> inventing one — the answer you can actually trust is the one that admits
> when there isn't one.

**Map the voice**
> Four sung prompts — lowest comfortable note, centre, top, and falsetto if
> you want it — and KeyGlo builds a range profile: comfortable, strong,
> extended. Save it per artist. Use it on every beat after that.

**Score the fit**
> Sing the hook. KeyGlo tracks the pitch live, then scores how much of that
> hook lands inside that artist's strong zone against that beat's key.
> Range Fit, Hook Match, Artist Fit.

**Move it, and hear it**
> It scores all nine transpositions and recommends one. You click it and
> hear it. It never applies anything on its own — the recommendation shows
> as a suggestion, and your track stays exactly as you left it until you
> choose.

## 4. The 808 section (own block — this converts on its own)

**Headline**
> Your 808 is four cents sharp and it's ruining the low end.

**Body**
> Drop any 808, one-shot or sample on the tuner. KeyGlo finds the stable
> sustain note — past the attack, where the pitch actually lives — tells you
> how many cents off it is, and recommends the move that puts it in your
> beat's scale.
>
> Hit **Solo** to hear the tuned version against the track. Hit **Apply
> Tune** and it writes a tuned copy next to the original. Your source file
> is never touched.
>
> If the sample glides instead of holding a note, it tells you that too —
> **Pitch Envelope: C1 → G1** — rather than averaging it into a number that
> means nothing.

## 5. Auto-Tune setup

> KeyGlo hands you the settings, you keep your workflow. It shows the key,

>
> *(Works alongside any tuner. KeyGlo isn't affiliated with Antares.)*

## 8. What it doesn't do (keep this section — it builds trust)

> KeyGlo doesn't tune your vocal. It's not a pitch corrector — it tells you
> what to set yours to.
>
> It doesn't write your melody or replace your ears. Every score is a
> measurement, and measurements are suggestions. If the −2 sounds worse to
> you, it is worse.
>
> The preview shifter is for auditioning, not for printing. Move the part in
> your DAW once you know where it belongs.

# Notes for whoever writes the page

- **Don't say "AI".** The footer badge says AI ANALYSIS because it shipped
  in the approved mockup, but the engines are signal processing — chroma
  templates, YIN pitch tracking, autocorrelation. Calling it AI invites a
  question we'd have to answer honestly.
- **Don't promise Auto-Tune integration.** KeyGlo displays settings for the
  user to enter. It does not drive third-party software.
- **Don't imply it fixes vocals.** It's diagnosis and recommendation.
- **The "no upload" claim is literal** and worth repeating — there is no
  server component at all.
- Screenshots must be the real interface, not the mockup: the shipping
  defaults are **Original / 0 cents**, not the mockup's −2 / +4.
